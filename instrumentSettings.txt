Wavefunction Sounds: settings that sound most like real instruments
====================================================================

Every instrument of a standard symphony orchestra, jazz big band and wind ensemble that has a
free recording, matched to the web app (python3 web/server.py) and available there as a preset.
Each was matched against up to three recorded notes across its range: the strengths of the first
20 harmonics (for drums and cymbals, the 1/3-octave spectrum) were compared with what every app
setting produces at the same pitches, across all seven potentials and every slider, and the
closest settings were refined and snapped to values the sliders can reach. "T = pitch" means
the temperature follows each note (higher notes brighter), which fits instruments whose
brightness changes that way across their range. "Match" is the RMS difference in dB averaged over
the notes: about 1-3 dB is very close, 5-7 dB recognisably similar, above 10 dB not really the
same sound. Envelopes are medians over the notes (attack = 10-90% rise / 0.8).

Limits of the model
-------------------
- Every pitched note is a periodic tone made of exact harmonics: no breath or bow noise, no
  vibrato, no piano inharmonicity, and no inharmonic overtones for bells, chimes or timpani. Drums
  and cymbals are mostly noise, which the app cannot make; their settings are the closest tones.
- One setting serves the whole range; a real instrument also changes character across it.
- The envelope is a simple ADSR, so swells and articulation inside a note are not modelled.
- Several quite different settings can sound about equally close ("Other potentials").
