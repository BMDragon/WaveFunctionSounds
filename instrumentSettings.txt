Wavefunction Sounds: settings that sound most like real instruments
====================================================================

Every instrument of a standard symphony orchestra, jazz big band and wind ensemble that has a
free recording, matched to the web app (python3 web/server.py) and available there as a preset.
Each was matched against up to eight recorded notes across its range (four recordings for drums
and cymbals). Recording and app were compared the way the ear hears them: each spectrum spread
along the ERB scale with filters one ERB wide, and the dB difference averaged with louder parts
of the spectrum counting more, plus twice the mismatch (in dB) of the waveform's crest factor
(peak / RMS), so that the shape of the wave counts and not only its spectrum: the same spectrum
can be a smooth wave or a buzzy pulse train ("Match", lower is closer: under 6 close, 6-10 fair,
above 14 poor). Every potential, slider, level weighting and sound the app has was searched
(hand-set weights and drawn potentials were not), the closest settings refined and snapped to
slider positions. "On ground scale" is the wavefunctions on the ground state's scale and
"Survival prob." the time-evolution sound. Envelopes are medians over the
notes. Attack = rise from 10% to 90% / 0.8, measured on each note's clean start: for bowed and
blown instruments up to the note's steady level, ignoring any later swell above it; for struck
and plucked ones up to the peak.

Limits of the model
-------------------
- The app makes tones from partials only: no breath or bow noise, no vibrato. Drums and cymbals
  are mostly noise, which the app cannot make; their settings are the closest tones.
- One setting serves the whole range; a real instrument also changes character across it.
- The envelope is a simple ADSR, so swells and articulation inside a note are not modelled.
- The match compares spectra the way the ear resolves them, but not everything the ear hears
  (attack transients, noise, beating); several settings can sound about equally close.

Community Edition, Philharmonia Orchestra sound samples, VCSL, MTG Solo Saxophones, Karoryfer
Black And Blue Basses, Virtuosity Drums and Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra.
