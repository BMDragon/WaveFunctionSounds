Wavefunction Sounds: settings that sound most like real instruments
====================================================================

For the web app (python3 web/server.py). Each instrument was matched to a real recording:
the strengths of its first 20 harmonics (for the snare drum, its 1/3-octave spectrum) were
compared with what every app setting produces at the same pitch, across all seven potentials
and every slider, and the closest settings were refined and snapped to values the sliders can
reach. "Match" is the RMS difference in dB over those 20 harmonics (anything below -60 dB counts
as silent): about 1-3 dB is very close, 5-7 dB is recognisably similar, above 10 dB is not
really the same sound.

Settings with Boltzmann weights use "Fixed temperature" mode, so the harmonic recipe is the same
on every key (temperature has no effect with Equal or 1 / level weights).
Envelope values come from the recordings: attack is the 10-90% rise / 0.8, decay is the fitted
exponential time constant, release is the fitted fall after the note ends.

Limits of the model
-------------------
- Every pitched note is a periodic tone made of exact harmonics, so there is no bow or breath
  noise, no vibrato, and no piano inharmonicity. The snare drum is mostly noise, which the app
  cannot produce; its closest setting (an inharmonic lattice tone) is still 12 dB away.
- Oboe and bassoon have a weak fundamental under strong 2nd-3rd harmonics. The double well
  weakens the fundamental, but not with the right balance of the harmonics above it, so these
  two still only match loosely (about 7 dB), best with the finite well.
- The lattice was only considered at Bloch phases 0 and pi for pitched instruments: the other
  phases make inharmonic partials, which real pitched instruments do not have.
- The envelope is attack + exponential decay + release with no sustain stage, so sustained
  instruments use the longest decay (6 s), and slow bowed swells are capped at a 300 ms attack.
- Several quite different settings can sound about equally close; these are the best found,
  not the only good ones ("Other potentials" shows how near each other potential gets).

Recordings: University of Iowa Electronic Music Studios, Musical Instrument Samples
(https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/MIS.html); harp and snare drum from the VSCO-2 Community
Edition library (https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE).
